About the department

Neurology & Neurosurgery research at Saint Luke's

The research department serves the neurologists, neurosurgeons, advanced practice providers, trainees and staff of Saint Luke's Health System in Kansas City, Missouri. Our clinical programs are part of Saint Luke's Marion Bloch Neuroscience Institute, and our research office supports investigators from first idea through IRB approval, data collection, analysis and publication.

Mission & values

Why we do this

Our mission is to improve the lives of people with neurological and neurosurgical conditions by generating evidence from the care we deliver, sharing it openly, and training clinicians who think like scientists.

Rigor

Prospective data, pre-specified analyses, IRB oversight and transparent reporting. Registries are designed before the first patient is enrolled.

Patients first

Research questions come from the clinic. We prioritize outcomes that matter to patients and families — function, independence and quality of life.

Collaboration

Neurology and neurosurgery work as one department, alongside nursing, rehabilitation, radiology, pathology, pharmacy and biostatistics.

Mentorship

Every project is a teaching opportunity. Trainees are authors, not observers, and learn the full arc of a study.

Stewardship

Patient data are handled under institutional policy and federal regulation, with access limited to approved personnel and protocols.

Openness

We publish what we learn, including negative results, and make our documentation and processes available to the whole department.

How research works here

Onboarding guide →
1

Idea

An investigator proposes a question, identifies a mentor if needed, and drafts a short concept in the Research Hub.

2

Admin review

The research office checks feasibility, data sources, statistical support and any budget or contract needs.

3

IRB

The protocol and consent documents are prepared and submitted. Projects that qualify use an umbrella IRB for registry studies.

4

Active → Publication

Data collection and analysis proceed under the approved protocol, ending in presentation and peer-reviewed publication.
